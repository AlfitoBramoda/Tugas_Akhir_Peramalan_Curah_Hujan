\chapter*{\textit{Abstract}}
Rainfall plays an important role in various sectors such as agriculture, infrastructure, and disaster risk management, making accurate prediction a primary necessity, especially in large cities like Surabaya that are vulnerable to flooding and waterlogging. 
Considering that rainfall is influenced by spatial and temporal variations simultaneously, this study applies the STARIMA model to predict monthly rainfall by accounting for dynamic interregional interactions. 
The data used are derived from NASA POWER satellite imagery for the period 2015-2024.
The research stages include data preprocessing through Box-Cox transformation and differencing, analysis of spatio-temporal patterns, stationarity testing, construction of a Spatial Weight Matrix using three weighting schemes (uniform, inverse distance, and cross-correlation), development of a SARIMA model as a benchmark, and performance evaluation using RMSE.
The results indicate the presence of consistent seasonal patterns and strong spatial correlations among regions in Surabaya, reflecting that spatial information cannot be ignored in rainfall forecasting. 
The best model is STARIMA$(0, 0, 0) × (0, 1, 1_{0})_{12}$ with spatial lag 1 and uniform weights, producing an average RMSE of 2.7934, which is lower than other STARIMA models as well as the SARIMA model. 
For example, in the eastern region, the RMSE decreases significantly from 4.634 (SARIMA) to 1.331 (STARIMA). 
These findings confirm that integrating spatial and temporal aspects through the STARIMA model improves rainfall prediction accuracy and has the potential to support early warning systems and hydrometeorological disaster mitigation in Surabaya.
