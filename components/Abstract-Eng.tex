\chapter*{\textit{Abstract}}
Rainfall plays a critical role in sectors such as agriculture, infrastructure, and disaster risk management, making accurate forecasting particularly essential in major urban areas like Surabaya, which is highly susceptible to flooding and surface inundation. 
Given that rainfall is simultaneously influenced by spatial and temporal variations, this study applies the spatio-temporal STARMA model to forecast monthly rainfall while accounting for the dynamic interdependencies among regions. 
The dataset utilized is sourced from NASA POWER satellite imagery for the period 2015 - 2024
The research workflow comprises data preprocessing through BoxCox transformation and differencing, spatio-temporal pattern analysis, stationarity testing, construction of Spatial Weight Matrices using three weighting schemes (uniform, inverse-distance, and cross-correlation), development of ARIMA models as benchmarks, and performance evaluation using RMSE. 
The results reveal consistent seasonal patterns and strong spatial correlations across Surabaya's regions, indicating that spatial information is indispensable in rainfall forecasting.
The best-performing model is identified as STARMA(1,1,1) with spatial lag 1 and its optimal configuration—uniform weighting—achieving an average RMSE of 1.728, outperforming both alternative STARMA models and ARIMA. 
In the East region, for instance, RMSE decreases markedly from 4.596 (ARIMA) to 1.407 (STARMA). 
These findings confirm that integrating spatial and temporal dimensions through STARMA substantially enhances rainfall prediction accuracy and holds significant potential to support early warning systems and hydrometeorological disaster mitigation efforts in Surabaya.
